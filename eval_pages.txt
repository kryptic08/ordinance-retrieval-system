Evaluation Tool 
The study utilized two distinct evaluation tools to assess both the technical accuracy of 
the retrieval and ranking components and the functional acceptability of the system. To evaluate 
the accuracy of OrdiSearch’s legal document retrieval, the researchers employed a Relevance 
Judgment Matrix, a retrieval-oriented adaptation of the confusion matrix. This tool compared the 
sections retrieved by the system against the relevance judgments assigned by human evaluators, 
who graded each retrieved provision using a four-point relevance scale: 3 for directly and 
completely relevant, 2 for substantially relevant, 1 for partially relevant, and 0 for not relevant. 
For the confusion matrix, sections with a grade greater than zero were treated as relevant, while 
sections with a grade of zero were treated as non-relevant. Based on this matrix, critical 
performance metrics such as Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1-Score were calculated. These 
metrics determined the reliability of the keyword, vector, and hybrid retrieval configurations in 
returning the correct ordinance sections, ensuring that the system met the technical standards 
required for a source-grounded legal retrieval tool. 
To assess the software’s quality from the user’s perspective, the study utilized a 
structured evaluation questionnaire based on the ISO/IEC 25010:2023 software quality 

=== PAGE 37 ===
standards. Municipal administrators, records officers, legal reviewers, and selected ordinance 
users rated the system using a five-point Likert scale. The weighted mean was the statistical 
method used to analyze this evaluation data. This method assigned different levels of importance 
to each response. In this approach, each rating provided by the evaluators was multiplied by a 
weight that reflected its significance. The resulting products were then summed and divided by 
the total of the weights. By applying this method, the study accounted for the varying influence 
of each response rather than treating all responses equally. As a result, the weighted mean 
provided a more comprehensive and meaningful interpretation of the collected data, reflecting 
the overall perception of the system’s quality and performance. 
Formula: 
M = Σ(w · x) / Σw 
Where: 
- x = numerical ratings provided by the evaluator, 
- w = assigned weights for each rating, and 
- M = computed weighted mean. 
F1 Score 
Along with the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality model, the system was also evaluated 
using the F1-Score. The F1-Score was a standard metric in information retrieval and machine 
learning used to measure the effectiveness of retrieval and classification systems. It provided a 
balanced evaluation of system performance by combining precision and recall, making it suitable 
for assessing collections with an uneven number of relevant and non-relevant provisions. In this 
study, the weighted average F1-score was utilized to reflect the performance of each query 

=== PAGE 38 ===
category based on its support, ensuring a more reliable measurement of the system’s retrieval 
capability. The formula used to calculate the F1-Score was: 
F1 = 2 × (Precision × Recall) / (Precision + Recall) 
Where: 
Precision = True Positives / (True Positives + False Positives) 
Recall = True Positives / (True Positives + False Negatives) 
- True Positives (TP) refer to retrieved ordinance sections that were judged relevant by 
the evaluators. 
- False Positives (FP) refer to retrieved sections that were judged non-relevant. 
- False Negatives (FN) refer to relevant sections that existed in the corpus but were not 
retrieved by the system. 
In addition to the F1-Score, the study measured Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) and 
Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain at k (nDCG@k) to evaluate ranking quality. 
MRR measured how quickly the first relevant section appeared in the result list, while 
nDCG@k accounted for the graded relevance of each result using the formula: 
DCG@k = Σ (2^(rel_i) - 1) / log2(i + 1) 
nDCG@k = DCG@k / IDCG@k 
where rel_i is the relevance grade of the section at rank i, and IDCG@k is the ideal 
discounted cumulative gain obtained by sorting all judged sections by descending 
relevance. The evaluation also measured retrieval latency, empty-retrieval behavior, and 
citation correctness. Citation correctness verified that each returned citation pointed to an 
existing chunk, the same document and section, and a valid page range.

dense vector cosine similarity, bilingual English–Filipino synonym expansion, legal stem 
normalization, topic coverage, section-heading alignment, exact phrase matching, and penalty or

system to distinguish between provisions that merely share generic legal wording and provisions

standardized retrieval and machine learning libraries, including Convex for keyword ranking and 
FastEmbed with the BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 model for semantic similarity, to ensure the 
reliability and consistency of the system’s retrieval evaluation.

Ethical Considerations

The researchers obtained formal approval to conduct the study at the Office of the 
Secretary to the Sangguniang Bayan of the Local Government Unit of Polangui, Albay, 
through appropriate institutional channels. Informed consent was secured from all 
participating administrative personnel, who were briefed on the study's objectives, scope,

standards. Responses from evaluation instruments were used solely for research purposes,

measures were implemented to secure, store, and properly dispose of all personal 
information. By following institutional and legal ethical standards, the study safeguarded 
the rights, privacy, and dignity of all participants involved in the development and 
evaluation of the VectorSearch-LGU system.
